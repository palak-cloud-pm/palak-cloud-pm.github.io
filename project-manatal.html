01 · Context & Brief

The assignment: make Manatal’s career page builder the best in its category

Manatal is a cloud-based recruitment platform used by staffing agencies and HR teams across Southeast Asia and beyond. Their career page builder lets companies create a branded jobs landing page connected to their Manatal ATS, but the feature had not kept pace with the market.

The assignment asked me to take on the role of a Senior PM owning this feature: audit the current product, benchmark it against the best in the market, identify the gaps, and design what the next version should look like.

Rather than produce a slide deck with recommendations, I built a working, deployed prototype of the improved builder, showing exactly what it would feel like to use, not just what it might look like on paper.

The Brief

Evaluate, benchmark, and improve

Audit the existing career page builder, identify the highest-value gaps versus competition, and design the next iteration with a clear product rationale.

My Approach

Show, don’t tell

Instead of recommendations in a deck, I shipped a live, clickable prototype demonstrating exactly what the improved builder would do, end to end.

02 · Competitive Analysis

Five platforms, one clear pattern

I evaluated Manatal against Workable, Greenhouse, Lever, Teamtailor, and BambooHR, focusing on four dimensions that directly affect the quality of a career page builder as a product: content creation, customisation, analytics, and platform integration.

Manatal had the ATS integration right. Everything on top of it was table stakes they were missing.

The pattern was unmistakable. Manatal’s ATS integration was solid, but modern career page builders had moved to AI-assisted content generation, rich WYSIWYG editing, and conversion analytics as baseline expectations. Manatal’s builder was functional, not differentiated.

03 · The Gaps

Four gaps worth actually fixing

01
No AI content generation
Companies, especially SMBs, struggle to write compelling employer brand copy. Every top competitor now offers AI-assisted generation from a brief. Manatal had none. This was the biggest perceived gap for new users.
02
No WYSIWYG editor
Editing required navigating separate settings screens. Users had no real-time visual feedback. The leading tools (Teamtailor, Workable) offered block-based drag-and-drop editing with instant preview, a dramatically better experience.
03
Thin template library
One or two layout options versus 10-20 on competing platforms. Companies in creative or consumer industries found the options limiting. Template variety signals brand credibility at the point of first impression.
04
No career page analytics
Manatal tracked applicants inside the ATS, but had no visibility into how the career page itself was performing: visitors, conversion rate, traffic sources, drop-off. This made it impossible for hiring managers to improve their recruitment funnel upstream of applications.
04 · What I Built

A working prototype: the career page builder, rebuilt

I built a fully functional, deployed prototype of the improved career page builder in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Every screen in the user journey is interactive and demonstrates the product experience, not just a wireframe or mockup.

Overview of the live career page: current theme, URL, open roles count, and quick access to the editor. Gives the user a sense of status before they edit.

05 · Key Product Decisions

The choices that shaped the design

AI-first, not AI-only. The builder offers both an AI-generated path and a manual path from the same entry point. The AI path answers the blank-page problem for most users, the manual path gives control to companies with existing brand guidelines. Forcing one removes the other’s customer.

Keep Manatal’s core advantage. The job sync from Manatal ATS is genuinely differentiated. Rather than rebuilding it, I made it more visible, adding a “Synced from Manatal” badge on role cards to reinforce platform integration as a selling point, not just a utility.

Analytics as a retention driver. Career page analytics give hiring managers a reason to return to the builder after publishing. Without it, the feature is a one-time setup task. With it, it becomes a live tool in the hiring workflow.

Prioritisation Framework

Impact vs. effort for each gap

AI generation and WYSIWYG editing were high-impact, medium-effort. Analytics were high-impact but required data infrastructure. Template library was medium-impact, low-effort. I sequenced accordingly.

Design Principle

Progressive disclosure throughout

The AI questionnaire reveals one question at a time. The editor reveals advanced options in the right panel only when a block is selected. Complexity is earned, not front-loaded.

06 · Reflection

What this project demonstrates

  • Competitive analysis as a product decision: I didn’t just list what competitors had. I turned the gaps into a prioritised feature set, grounded in user impact and Manatal’s existing strengths. The analysis shaped the prototype, not the other way around.
  • Shipping over pitching: A working prototype communicates a product vision more precisely than any deck. It reveals edge cases, interaction logic, and UX decisions that slide bullets cannot. I build to think, not to present.
  • AI-native PM execution: Built end-to-end solo using Claude Code. No engineers, no designers, no agency. From competitive brief to live URL. This is the productivity unlock AI tools give a PM who knows how to use them.
  • Platform thinking: The best product decision wasn’t adding features. It was identifying which existing feature (ATS job sync) was already the moat, and making it more visible. Good PMs amplify what’s already working before stacking new complexity on top.
  • Metric-first feature design: Every feature in the prototype traces back to a measurable outcome: time to first published page (AI generation), return usage rate (analytics), employer brand quality (templates + WYSIWYG). No features for features’ sake.
